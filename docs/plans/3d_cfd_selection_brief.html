A practical route to fast 3D RDE CFD

Decision brief | 24 October 2026 | 3d-modelling | Proposal only; no new solver work or compute jobs

Recommend PeleC on Modal GPUs, conditionally. Reuse the existing C++/AMReX/PelePhysics integration; retain installed detonationFoam as the CPU fallback. Both have zero license fees. Our H100 execution works, but the current planar control is 15-18% too fast and fails reaction-structure checks. Full GRI chemistry is a reference, not a demonstrated fast production recipe. Current evidence.

Target milliseconds, then slow the movie. Propose 0-2 ms of simulated startup/circulation initially, extending to 5-8 ms if settling requires it. At our reference speed a wave circles in about 0.126 ms: 2 ms covers about 16 revolutions. One physical second costs roughly 500 times a 2 ms run at unchanged workload. A 10-30 second animation can show those milliseconds; it must display the actual physical clock. Two-hour completion is a target, not established feasibility.

First model: gaseous methane/oxygen, thermally perfect ideal-gas species, FP64, full 3D annulus and core-end expansion. Preserve temperature-dependent thermodynamics and finite-rate kinetics; omit droplets, radiation and solid-wall thermal evolution initially. An equivalent premixed inlet and finite ignition pulse can show wave establishment in that idealized model. Predicting the real injection-to-ignition sequence requires separate jets and the predetonator. Mundt is the experimental reference; the CFD papers below are separate studies.

What the papers justify

Argonne / Pal 2021 used CONVERGE 2.4, separate injectors, a predetonator, LES/AMR and 12 species/37 reactions: reuse the modeling strategy, not a presumed runtime. NETL / Strakey 2022 used Fluent and 12 species/38 reactions; coarsening 200 to 400 micrometres changed two waves to one. Senga 2024 / charLES reports 6 ms in 11.2/19.36 hours on four A100s for 3.56/7.58M cells with extended flamelet chemistry; wave speeds 1224/1113 m/s versus 1668 measured. Timing followed a startup/restart procedure, not an end-to-end cold start. This supports an overnight possibility, not guaranteed fidelity or PeleC throughput.

Proposed next work after review: resolve the existing planar propagation defect using supported native numerics; obtain and screen traceable FFCMy-12 from the RDE literature (not FFCM1_Red). Check refill and finite ignition, then benchmark a representative reacting/geometry case on 1, 2, 4 and optionally 8 GPUs. Select the cheapest allocation meeting a measured forecast: <90 minutes integration plus queue/output margin for the two-hour target, or <10 hours plus margin overnight. Full-engine feasibility remains open; do not launch dependent runs while propagation fails.

Deliverable: case inputs/geometry → native CFD → pressure, heat-release and fresh-gas fields → 3D cutaway animation with physical timestamps and front tracks. Start at a declared injection/ignition state; later resolve the real predetonator if cold-start formation is required. Reuse established chemistry integration: the expensive part includes stiff per-cell ODEs and many tiny shock-resolving steps. Adding another general PDE library will not remove those costs.
